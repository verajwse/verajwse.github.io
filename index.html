What is fika?

If there is one Swedish tradition I would love to share, it's fika. Many countries have coffee breaks, but fika is hard to translate into other languages. It's about much more than coffee and pastries. It's about taking a moment to slow down, enjoy good company, and spend time together.

bakluckefika "Tailgate fika": coffee, tea, and homemade treats around the trunk of a car.

Fika is also an important part of our ministry. After a morning of preaching, it's common to take a break together, enjoy some coffee and something sweet, and encourage one another. We even gather around the trunk of a car for bakluckefika before continuing in the ministry.

Since I love baking, fika has always been one of my favorite Swedish traditions. To give you a small taste of Sweden, I've included some of my favorite recipes here. I hope you enjoy making them as much as I do!

Cinnamon bunsKanelbullar About 25 buns
2 hours

Dough

  • 75 g butter (⅓ cup)
  • 2½ dl milk (1 cup)
  • 25 g fresh yeast (or 1 packet / 7 g dry yeast)
  • ½ dl sugar (¼ cup)
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • about 7 dl all-purpose flour (about 3½ cups)

Filling

  • 75 g soft butter (⅓ cup)
  • ½ dl sugar (¼ cup)
  • 1 tbsp ground cinnamon

Topping

  • 1 egg, beaten
  • Pearl sugar (or coarse sugar)

How to make them

  1. Melt the butter, add the milk and warm it to about 37 °C (98 °F), just finger-warm.
  2. Crumble the yeast into a bowl and stir in a little of the warm milk until it dissolves. Add the rest of the milk, the sugar, salt and cardamom.
  3. Add most of the flour and knead for about 10 minutes until the dough is smooth and only slightly sticky. Add more flour if needed.
  4. Cover and let it rise for about 40 minutes.
  5. Roll the dough out into a rectangle, about 30 × 40 cm (12 × 16 in). Spread the soft butter over it and sprinkle with sugar and cinnamon.
  6. Roll it up from the long side and cut into about 25 slices. Place them in paper cups or on a baking tray.
  7. Let them rise for another 30 minutes. Brush with egg and sprinkle with pearl sugar.
  8. Bake in the middle of the oven at 225 °C (435 °F) for 8–10 minutes until golden.

Swedes celebrate Kanelbullens dag, Cinnamon Bun Day, every year on 4 October.

Sticky chocolate cakeKladdkaka 8 slices
30 minutes

Ingredients

  • 100 g butter (7 tbsp)
  • 2 eggs
  • 2½ dl sugar (1 cup)
  • 1½ dl all-purpose flour (⅔ cup)
  • 4 tbsp cocoa powder
  • 1 tsp vanilla sugar (or ½ tsp vanilla extract)
  • A pinch of salt

How to make it

  1. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Butter a round cake pan, about 22–24 cm (9 in), and dust it with breadcrumbs or cocoa.
  2. Melt the butter and let it cool slightly.
  3. Whisk the eggs and sugar together just until combined. Don't whip it fluffy.
  4. Stir in the flour, cocoa, vanilla and salt, then the melted butter.
  5. Pour into the pan and bake for 15–20 minutes. The middle should still be soft and sticky.
  6. Let it cool completely. It sets as it cools.

Serve with whipped cream or vanilla ice cream, and fresh berries if you have them.

Chocolate ballsChokladbollar About 20 balls
No baking

Ingredients

  • 100 g soft butter (7 tbsp)
  • 1 dl sugar (scant ½ cup)
  • 2 tsp vanilla sugar (or 1 tsp vanilla extract)
  • 3 tbsp cocoa powder
  • 3 dl rolled oats (1¼ cups)
  • 2 tbsp cold coffee
  • Desiccated coconut or pearl sugar, for rolling

How to make them

  1. Mix the butter, sugar, vanilla and cocoa until smooth.
  2. Stir in the oats and the coffee.
  3. Roll into small balls and roll them in coconut or pearl sugar.
  4. Chill in the fridge for at least 30 minutes before serving.

Kids in Sweden often make these as their very first "baking." No oven needed.
